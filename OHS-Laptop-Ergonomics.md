# Occupational Health & Safety (OHS) for Desk-Based Work

## Research

### Risks of using a laptop without external peripherals
Working directly on a laptop for long periods can lead to neck and upper‑back strain from constantly looking down. It can also cause wrist and shoulder discomfort due to cramped typing positions, and eye strain if the screen is too low or too close.

### Ergonomic equipment that helps
- An external monitor placed at eye level helps keep my head upright and reduces neck strain.
- An external keyboard and mouse allow my arms and wrists to stay in a more natural, relaxed position.
- A laptop stand or riser lifts the screen higher when an external monitor is not available.

### Workspace adjustments
- Position the top of the monitor at or slightly below eye level.
- Keep the screen about an arm’s length away so it is easy to view without leaning forward.
- Sit with feet flat on the floor and knees bent at about 90 degrees.
- Place the keyboard and mouse close enough so my elbows stay near my sides.

### Daily habits that reduce strain
- Take a short break every 45–60 minutes to stand, stretch, or walk around.
- Check my posture regularly and straighten up if I start to slouch.

## Reflection

### Equipment changes I can make
I can add a laptop stand to raise my screen closer to eye level and use an external keyboard and mouse when working at my desk. If I work somewhere else, I can place the laptop on a stack of books and use a portable keyboard.

### Behavioural changes I can implement
I will set a timer or use Focus Bear to remind me to stand and stretch every hour. I will also pay attention to my posture and avoid working from the couch for long periods.

### Reminders for posture and breaks
I plan to use Focus Bear’s reminders to take movement‑break during a work session. Taking a short walk or stretch break every hour will help me reset my posture.
